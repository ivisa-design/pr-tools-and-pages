What is Umrah?

Umrah is an Islamic pilgrimage to Makkah (Mecca), Saudi Arabia. Unlike Hajj — which takes place during a fixed period in the Islamic lunar calendar and is one of the five pillars of Islam — Umrah can be performed at any time of year (outside the Hajj blackout window) and is considered a highly recommended but non-obligatory act of worship.

The pilgrimage involves wearing the Ihram, performing Tawaf (circling the Kaaba seven times counterclockwise), walking between the hills of Safa and Marwa seven times (Sa'i), and shaving or cutting the hair. Many pilgrims also visit Al-Masjid an-Nabawi (the Prophet's Mosque) in Madinah. Umrah is exclusively for Muslims. All travelers require either a dedicated Umrah visa or a Saudi tourist eVisa (which allows Umrah outside the Hajj season).

Non-Muslim visitors: Non-Muslims cannot perform Umrah or enter Makkah under any circumstances. You can visit Saudi Arabia on a tourist eVisa and explore cities like Riyadh, Jeddah, and AlUla — but Makkah remains off-limits regardless of visa type.

Eligibility — Am I Eligible?

Your eligibility for Umrah depends on four key factors. Work through each:

1. Are you Muslim?

Umrah is exclusively for Muslims. Both the Umrah visa and the pilgrimage itself require the Islamic faith. If you're a convert, see the Muslim Converts section in the FAQ.

2. Is your passport valid for at least 6 months?

Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months from your intended travel date, with at least 2 blank pages for stamps. If not, renew your passport first — allow 4–6 weeks for renewal before applying.

3. What is your nationality?

US / UK / EU / Australia / New Zealand / Malaysia / Singapore / Turkey and 30+ others: Eligible for both Umrah visa and Saudi tourist eVisa. Can apply directly on nusuk.sa. Processing: 4–5 working days (Umrah eVisa) / 24–48 hours (Tourist eVisa).
India / Pakistan / Bangladesh: Eligible for Umrah visa via a licensed travel agent only (MOIA-registered or Saudi-approved). Cannot self-apply on Nusuk. iVisa is an approved agent partner. Processing: 4–5 working days.
GCC nationals (UAE, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman, Qatar): Eligible with dedicated GCC access on Nusuk. Specialized pre-booking windows, no travel agency required. Processing: typically within 24 hours.
Saudi nationals: No visa required. Free access to holy sites year-round. Register on Nusuk to book your Umrah permit.
Other nationalities: Most countries are eligible for the Umrah visa via a licensed agent. If you hold a valid residency permit for the UK, EU, Canada, or USA, you may also qualify for the Saudi tourist eVisa even if your home country is not on the standard list. Contact iVisa to confirm your specific path.

4. Do you have your meningitis vaccination?

Saudi Arabia requires a meningococcal ACWY vaccine for all pilgrims. It must be administered at least 10 days before arrival and be within its validity period. The vaccine is NOT part of your visa application — it's a travel health requirement checked on entry. Keep your certificate with your travel documents. Conjugate ACWY vaccine is valid for 5 years; polysaccharide for 3 years. Contact your local travel clinic — allow at least 14 days to be safe.

There's nothing preventing eligible travelers from using the tourist eVisa if they prefer broader Saudi access.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Umrah?

Umrah is an Islamic pilgrimage to Makkah (Mecca), Saudi Arabia. Unlike Hajj, which takes place during a fixed period and is one of the five pillars of Islam, Umrah can be performed at any time of year (outside the Hajj blackout window) and is considered a highly recommended but non-obligatory act of worship.

The pilgrimage involves wearing Ihram, performing Tawaf (circling the Kaaba seven times counterclockwise), walking between the hills of Safa and Marwa seven times (Sa'i), and shaving or cutting the hair. Many pilgrims also visit Al-Masjid an-Nabawi (the Prophet's Mosque) in Madinah. Approximately 2 million people perform Umrah each year, with Ramadan being the most popular period.

Can non-Muslims visit Makkah or perform Umrah?

No. Umrah is an act of Islamic worship exclusively for Muslims. Non-Muslims are strictly and permanently prohibited from entering Makkah under any circumstances — including tourism, transit, business, or journalism. This is Saudi law, enforced through physical checkpoints on every road leading into the holy sanctuary. Holding a Saudi tourist eVisa does not grant access to Makkah.

What about Madinah? Non-Muslims can visit the city of Madinah and stay in hotels, but cannot enter the interior of Al-Masjid an-Nabawi (the Prophet's Mosque). Muslim travelers on a tourist eVisa can perform Umrah — both visa types permit entry into Makkah for Muslim travelers.

Can I perform Umrah on a tourist visa?

Yes — the Saudi Ministry of Hajj and Umrah allows tourist visa holders to perform Umrah at any time of year, except during the Hajj season (when tourist visa holders are turned back at Makkah checkpoints). You must also register on Nusuk regardless of your visa type — Saudi authorities check the Nusuk app at checkpoints around the Grand Mosque. Without an active Nusuk permit, you can be removed from the area and fined, even with a valid visa.

The tourist eVisa gives more flexibility: you can book flights, hotels, and transport independently, visit other Saudi cities like Jeddah and Riyadh, and the visa is valid for 1 year with multiple entries and up to 90 days per stay.

Can women travel without a male guardian (mahram)?

Yes. Saudi Arabia removed the mahram requirement for women aged 18 and over for Umrah purposes in 2021. Women can apply for and travel on an Umrah or tourist visa independently, without a mahram letter or guardian consent form. Some travel agents in South Asia may still request a mahram letter for their own compliance — this is not an official Saudi government requirement. The no-mahram rule applies to Umrah; Hajj has separate guidance reviewed each season by the Saudi Ministry.

What's the difference between an Umrah visa and a tourist eVisa?

The Umrah visa is for Muslims only — it includes mandatory health insurance, covers Makkah and Madinah, and allows travel to other Saudi cities after completing Umrah via Nusuk Masar. It's purpose-built for pilgrimage with a 30-day validity and seasonal deadlines.

The tourist eVisa is open to all eligible nationalities, allows multiple entries for 1 year (up to 90 days per stay), covers all Saudi cities, and is simpler to apply for — it also includes health insurance (certificate issued separately) — but tourist eVisa holders are blocked from Makkah during Hajj season (~April–June).

What is Nusuk and do I need it?

Nusuk (nusuk.sa) is the official Saudi government platform. It serves as a visa portal for eligible nationalities, a ritual permit booking system (mandatory for all pilgrims regardless of visa type), and a hotel/transport platform. Everyone uses it at some stage — either to apply for the visa directly, or to book permits after approval through an agent. Without a Nusuk permit, you cannot perform Umrah rituals at the Grand Mosque, even with a valid visa.

Do I need Nusuk to get my Umrah visa?

It depends on your nationality. US, UK, EU, GCC, Malaysia, Indonesia, and many other nationalities can apply directly at nusuk.sa. India, Pakistan, and Bangladesh must use a licensed travel agent — they cannot self-apply on Nusuk. iVisa can confirm your specific path and handle the full process on your behalf.

How long does processing take?

The Umrah visa takes 4–5 working days for most nationalities (both Nusuk-direct and agent-required). The tourist eVisa processes in 24–48 hours (sometimes instant). During Ramadan, add 2–3 extra days for Umrah visas and 1–2 days for tourist eVisas. Apply at least 2 weeks before travel, or 3–4 weeks during Ramadan. Timings begin once all required documents are submitted correctly — incomplete applications reset the clock.

Do I need a meningitis vaccine?

Yes. The meningococcal ACWY vaccine is required for all Umrah pilgrims. It must be administered at least 10 days before your arrival in Saudi Arabia and must still be within its validity period. The vaccine is NOT part of your visa application — you do not submit your certificate when applying. It is a travel health requirement checked on entry into Saudi Arabia. Keep your certificate with your travel documents. The conjugate ACWY vaccine is valid for 5 years; the polysaccharide vaccine is valid for 3 years. Contact your local travel clinic and allow at least 14 days before departure.

Can I extend my Umrah visa in Saudi Arabia?

Extensions are not offered for Umrah visas. The visa is valid for 30 days from the issue date and the seasonal departure deadline (April 7, 2027) overrides individual validity. If you need more time in Saudi Arabia — for tourism, visiting family, or exploring other cities — a tourist eVisa is the better option: valid for 1 year with multiple entries and up to 90 days per stay.

What if my application is rejected?

Rejections are rare and usually caused by incomplete documents, incorrect information, or applying during the Hajj blackout. Can I take Zamzam water home?

Umrah visa holders: yes, 5 litres. Purchase via the Nusuk app at the airport (official channel only) and pack it in your checked luggage. Tourist eVisa holders should confirm with their airline before travel — policies vary by carrier. Zamzam water cannot be bought from street vendors; the only official source is the designated airport kiosks linked to the Nusuk platform.

I'm a Muslim convert — how do I prove my faith?

Get a letter from your local mosque confirming your conversion to Islam. Most mosques provide this free of charge or for a small donation — it should be on mosque letterhead and signed by the Imam. There's no requirement to change your legal name. A signed personal declaration can also work as a backup, but a mosque letter is always the safer option. iVisa has handled many convert applications and can guide you through the specific documentation needed.

Can I visit other Saudi cities on an Umrah visa?

Yes — after completing your Umrah rituals, you can visit other Saudi cities including Jeddah, Riyadh, Taif, and AlUla on the same Umrah visa. Book hotel stays in other cities via Nusuk Masar. All travel must be within your 30-day visa window and you must depart by April 7, 2027. For more flexible Saudi travel, the tourist eVisa (valid 1 year, multiple entries) is the better option.

When can't I apply for an Umrah visa?

During the Hajj season blackout — approximately March 15 to May 31 of the Hajj year. Applications submitted during this period will be refused. For the 2026/2027 season, the application window closes on March 9, 2027. Ramadan (approximately February 17 – March 18, 2027) is peak season — visas are still issued but expect delays and significantly higher hotel prices near the Grand Mosque.
